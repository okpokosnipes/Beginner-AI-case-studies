AI in Autonomous Driving: From Perception to Decision-Making

Independent AI Analysis and Evaluation Case Study

Project Overview

This independent case study examines how artificial intelligence (AI) can be applied to autonomous driving through perception, prediction, decision-making, and action.

The project explores how an autonomous driving system may interpret its environment, anticipate events, select appropriate responses, and execute driving actions. It also examines potential system failures and approaches to evaluating safety and performance.

Objectives

- Explain the major functions of AI in autonomous driving.
- Distinguish between perception, prediction, decision-making, and action.
- Examine the role of autonomy and adaptability.
- Identify potential risks and limitations.
- Develop a conceptual framework for evaluating AI system performance and safety.

AI System Functions

1. Perception

The system identifies and interprets information from its environment, such as:

- Traffic lights
- Road signs
- Pedestrians
- Other vehicles
- Road conditions
- Obstacles

Key question: What is happening in the environment?

2. Prediction

The system estimates what may happen next based on available information.

Examples include predicting:

- Whether a pedestrian may cross the road.
- Whether another vehicle may change lanes.
- How traffic conditions may develop.

Key question: What might happen next?

3. Decision-Making

The system selects an appropriate response based on its perception, predictions, traffic rules, and safety requirements.

Examples include:

- Slowing down.
- Stopping for a pedestrian.
- Changing lanes when appropriate.
- Selecting a suitable route.

Key question: What should the system do?

4. Action

The system executes its selected response through vehicle controls such as:

- Steering
- Braking
- Acceleration

Key question: Did the system carry out the intended action correctly?

Autonomy and Adaptability

Autonomy

Autonomy refers to the system's ability to perform tasks and make operational decisions without continuous human instruction, while operating within defined constraints.

Adaptability

An AI system may be designed to improve or adjust its performance based on data or changing conditions. However, adaptation does not necessarily mean that the system continues learning autonomously after deployment.

Potential Benefits

- Improved efficiency in transportation and logistics.
- Potential reduction in certain types of human driving errors.
- More efficient route planning.
- Improved mobility for some users.

Potential Limitations and Risks

- High development and deployment costs.
- Difficulty handling rare or unexpected situations.
- Performance challenges in adverse weather conditions.
- Safety risks when system decisions or controls fail.
- Ethical challenges involving unavoidable high-risk situations.
- Dependence on sensors, software, infrastructure, and reliable data.

Failure Analysis

A driving incident may result from different stages of an AI system.

For example, if a pedestrian is present but the vehicle fails to stop, possible causes include:

Stage| Possible Failure
Perception| The pedestrian was not detected correctly.
Prediction| The system incorrectly estimated the pedestrian's movement or risk.
Decision-making| The system identified the risk but selected an inappropriate response.
Action/Control| The system selected the correct response, but the braking or control mechanism failed to execute it properly.

The final outcome alone does not establish the root cause. Evidence from the different stages of the system would be required to determine where the failure originated.

Conceptual Evaluation Framework

An autonomous driving system could be evaluated using criteria such as:

- Object and pedestrian detection accuracy.
- Response time.
- Performance under different weather conditions.
- Compliance with traffic rules.
- Decision-making in unexpected scenarios.
- Reliability of braking and steering controls.
- Performance across different road and traffic conditions.
- Consistency and reliability of system responses.

Evaluation should examine not only the final outcome but also the individual stages of the system.
